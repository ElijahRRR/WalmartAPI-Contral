## Overview

Products subject to our policy on Electronics and Radio Frequency Devices, Drones, Unmanned aerial systems (UAS) and Streaming devices must also adhere to other [Prohibited Products Policies](https://marketplacelearn.walmart.com/guides/Policies%20&%20standards/Prohibited%20products%20&%20brands/Prohibited-products-policy:-overview) or Walmart will automatically unpublish your item.

## Electronics and radio frequency devices

## What is the policy?

Walmart prohibits the sale of radio frequency devices that aren’t authorized by the Federal Communication Commission (FCC). In addition, Walmart prohibits radio frequency devices that, although authorized, are operating in a manner inconsistent with that authorization. Items listed on Walmart’s Marketplace must contain all device labeling, information and warnings required by the FCC.

### Additional information

This policy applies to devices that emit—whether intentionally, unintentionally, or incidentally—radio frequency energy. Because these devices can put out electrical signals that interfere with important communications channels, their design, manufacture and sale are subject to regulation by the FCC. Radio frequency devices that aren’t appropriately authorized by the FCC or that are otherwise not in compliance with FCC rules are prohibited from sale on Walmart Marketplace.

## Drones and Unmanned Aerial Systems

## What is the policy?

All foreign made Unmanned Aerial Systems (UAS) such as drones along with foreign-made UAS critical components are prohibited from sale on Walmart unless they were granted FCC authorization prior to December 2025 that remains valid or are subject to a specific authorization granted by the federal government.

### Additional information

Drones listed on Walmart Marketplace must include the valid FCC ID associated with the product in the key features or product description in the listing as well as an image of the FCC ID appearing on the product for sale.

## Streaming devices

## What is the policy?

You may list streaming devices on Walmart’s Marketplace only if the devices comply with all applicable Federal Communications Commission (FCC) requirements and are described and marketed in a manner that doesn’t facilitate or encourage infringement, piracy or other unlawful behavior. Listings must include the device’s FCC ID prominently within the product detail page and in at least one clear product or packaging image.

### Additional information

This policy applies to all listings on Walmart’s Marketplace for hardware or accessories with primary or material function to stream, cast or otherwise access audio-visual content over the internet (collectively, “Streaming Devices”). Streaming devices are subject to FCC regulation and other applicable laws due to their communication capabilities.

Walmart reserves the right to remove any item at its discretion, including where customer feedback indicates seller misconduct or reports from brand or intellectual property owners.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a Radio Frequency Device?

A [radio frequency device](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-2/subpart-I/section-2.801) is any device that is capable of emitting radio frequency energy by radiation, conduction, or other means. Many electronic and electrical products are radio frequency devices.

### What are the types of Radio Frequency Devices?

Radio frequency devices are grouped under the following categories:

- **Intentional radiators**:

Devices that intentionally generate and emit radio frequency energy are considered "[Intentional Radiators"](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-15/subpart-A/section-15.3) by the FCC. Examples of Intentional Radiators include devices with cellular, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or other wireless connectivity such as mobile phones, two-way radios, wireless microphones, radio-controlled toys, universal remote-control devices, wireless alarm systems, drones, signal boosters, and FM radio transmitters.

- **Unintentional radiators:**

Devices that aren’t intended to emit radio frequency energy, but that intentionally generate radio frequency energy for use within the device or to send signals to associated equipment via connected wiring are considered "[Unintentional Radiators"](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-15/subpart-A/section-15.3) by the FCC. Examples of Unintentional Radiators include TV and radio receivers, garage door receivers, personal computers and computer peripherals, smart battery chargers, and LED signs.

- **Incidental radiators:**

Devices that aren’t designed or intended to do so, but nevertheless still generate radio frequency energy because of operations are considered "[Incidental Radiators"](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-15/subpart-A/section-15.3) by the FCC. Examples of Incidental Radiators include hair dryers, alternating current (AC) and direct current (DC) motors, mechanical light switches, and basic electrical power tools that do not contain digital logic.

### What kind of authorization does the FCC require for marketing radio frequency devices?

Compliance requirements for marketing radio frequency devices are as follows:

- Intentional and Unintentional Radiators are required to be authorized by the FCC 1 prior to being marketed in the United States. Intentional Radiators generally require certification, while Unintentional Radiators may be authorized under the “Supplier’s Declaration of Conformity" (SDoC) process. If a device is both an Intentional and Unintentional Radiator, both processes may apply.

- Incidental Radiators don’t need to be authorized but must still comply with relevant FCC requirements.

- To be certified by the FCC, Intentional Radiators must be tested for compliance at a lab that has been accredited by the FCC, and an application for an equipment authorization (certification) must be submitted. Once a device is certified, the FCC issues an FCC ID number and label, and publishes the device’s technical information on a publicly accessible database.

- To comply with the [SDoC procedure](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-2/subpart-J/subject-group-ECFR1d1c20dde929672/section-2.906) for Unintentional Radiators, the responsible party for the device — who must be located in the United States— must have the item tested for compliance, maintain records demonstrating compliance, and provide such records to the FCC upon request.

All types of radio frequency devices must include required labels, warnings, and information for the user. Additionally, all radio frequency devices must comply with the FCC’s rules regarding [radio frequency radiation exposure limits](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/1.1310).

### What should I do if changes were made to an authorized radio frequency device?

If changes are made to an authorized radio frequency device, the responsible party must either ensure that the change complies with the FCC’s [permissive change rules](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-2/subpart-J/subject-group-ECFR55475dd0ca1a058/section-2.1043) or have the device reauthorized. This includes changes to the antenna, hardware or software changes, changes to the device enclosure, or changes in radio frequency exposure considerations.

### What does it mean to “display the FCC ID” on the listing?

The FCC ID must appear in the product detail page description and be visible in at least one image showing the product or its packaging.

### May I describe functionality for general streaming if my device can run third-party apps?

Yes, so long as your listing doesn’t promote or enable piracy, geo-lock circumvention, video game emulation, or other infringement, and the device otherwise complies with applicable FCC requirements and disclosure obligations. Marketing that misleads customers or encourages misuse is prohibited.

### What happens if customers or brand or IP owners report that a device is being used for piracy or emulation?

Walmart may remove the listing or take other actions deemed appropriate in its discretion, including where customer feedback indicates potential seller misconduct or rights-holder concerns.
